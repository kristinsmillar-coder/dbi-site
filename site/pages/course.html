After school · Led by industry professionals

Four hours.
A working view of business.

Flagship sessions meet after school, typically once a month for a four-hour afternoon. Founders, investors, and operators teach from the work they do every day.

Students hear how a business decision gets made, try it themselves in a workshop, and bring their questions back to the professional who taught it. Dinner, conversation, and time with their team are part of the afternoon.

An example from the room

Ten chips. Three choices.

Your team runs a five-location boba chain. You have ten investment chips and twelve possible investments. Choose exactly three, name two things you are choosing to be bad at, then defend the trade-off to a board member.

“What did you give up, and why was that okay?”

Fall capstone · Advise a real company

The Case Competition

The company brings a decision it actually needs to make.

Teams apply what they have learned in finance, strategy, operations, marketing, and communication to a real business problem. They research the market, weigh the alternatives, build a financial case, and defend a recommendation to company leadership and judges.

Students are acting as advisers to an existing business. The goal is a credible recommendation, supported by evidence and presented clearly under questioning.

Inside the 2025 case

Where should Surfline expand?

Eight teams of five evaluated Indonesia, South Africa, and Brazil. The winning team recommended Indonesia, combining an AI trip-planning concept called WAVES with hardware and coaching services. They presented their strategy and financial model to Surfline’s own leadership.

The step between semesters

Progression is earned.

Completing the fall does not automatically secure a place in entrepreneurship. Current Flagship students who want to continue submit a venture pitch and interview again. This internal selection is separate from the fall application window for new students.

Spring finale · Build your own company

The Dons
Startup Summit

Months of building become one pitch.

Selected students develop a venture with guidance from industry entrepreneurs and USD MBA mentors. They work on the customer problem, product, business model, and the case for why their company should succeed.

The Summit brings that work to the stage. Teams pitch their own companies to an experienced judging panel, with families and the school community in the audience. Judges evaluate the ventures, and the audience chooses a People’s Choice winner.

Awards support the next stage of building. At the May 19, 2026 Summit, 19 founders representing 10 companies competed for $19,000 in awards, including technology and professional shadowing opportunities.
